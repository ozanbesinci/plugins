# Scoring, contract and supplier-risk rules

## Hard gate before score

Apply mandatory administrative and technical conditions before weighted scoring. A verified mandatory non-compliance can exclude an offer. Missing information is not automatic exclusion; keep it conditional and create an RFI.

- One eligible supplier: do not create comparative weighted scoring.
- Two suppliers: ratio normalization is allowed; do not use min-max range scoring because it forces 10/0 regardless of gap.
- Seven or more suppliers: hard-gate and cost all offers, then apply detailed weighted scoring to a documented shortlist when appropriate.

## No double counting

A factor monetized in the comparable total cost must not be penalized again as the same thing in weighted scoring. Examples: payment timing, quantified missing scope, operating energy, and priced maintenance. Score only the residual qualitative risk that was not monetized.

Numeric criteria use formulas, not discretionary 1-10 values. Subjective criteria require a written reason and editable status. Missing subjective data uses a neutral placeholder only if the method explicitly allows it, and always creates an RFI.

Total score stays on one declared scale. Default v5 analytical helpers use 0-10. Calculate the distinguishing-weight ratio: criteria that give every supplier the same score do not distinguish the decision. Below 30%, do not present weighted scoring alone as a decision rationale; 30-60% is weak; 60% or higher means weight separation is better but does not certify data quality.

Always present weighted score, comparable total cost ranking, and hard-gate/red-line status as separate axes. If they point to different suppliers, state the conflict rather than forcing one winner.

## Supplier and contract risk

Assess corporate/financial capacity, references, manufacturing/site capacity, service coverage, workload, subcontractor structure, origin/single-source exposure, and probability x impact risk where evidence exists.

Contract review covers at least: delay damages and cap, extension rules, guarantees and release, price revision, change-order mechanism, risk/title transfer, acceptance, warranty, liability limits, force majeure, IP/control passwords where relevant, HSE/social-security obligations, subcontractor permission, and dispute venue.

Do not invent a guarantee, insurance, penalty, or percentage. Separate the source condition from any proposed negotiation point and explain the evidence/assumption needed for a quantified recommendation.
